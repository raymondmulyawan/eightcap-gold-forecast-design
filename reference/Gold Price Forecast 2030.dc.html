Before you read

No one knows where gold will be in 2030. Most banks only publish targets for the next 12 to 24 months, and the 2030 figures you see quoted are scenarios rather than a consensus. Every gold price prediction on this page is dated and labelled as a base case, bull scenario or third-party estimate.

Remember: forecasts are opinions that change often. Treat every number here as general information, not advice.

How targets changed in 2026

In early July, JPMorgan cut its Q4 2026 target to $4,500 from about $6,000, citing weaker demand from key buyers and gold's greater sensitivity to real rates, while keeping a positive medium-term view.

Goldman Sachs moved its end-2026 target from $5,400 in January to $4,650 in September, and kept $5,400 for end-2027. Forecasts changed a lot this year, which is a reason to treat long-range numbers carefully.

What forecasts say about 2030

JPMorgan's $8,000 figure for the end of the decade is a bull scenario from 29 January 2026, not its base case. It depends on private investors lifting their gold allocations from about 3% to 4.6%. Incrementum's In Gold We Trust 2026 report points to about $8,900 by the end of 2030.

The spread between the lowest and highest forecasts is wide, and that is the point. Long-range gold figures describe what could happen if certain conditions hold, not what will happen.

Gold sentiment in the news

Views are split. BCA sees gold as oversold below $4,000 but doubts a quick rally (26 October). Sucden warns of a drop toward $3,900 if the Fed keeps tightening (28 October), while State Street says a path to $5,000 remains even as downside risks build (27 October).
